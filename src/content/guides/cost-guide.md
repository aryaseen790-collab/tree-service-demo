## The biggest factors in tree removal cost

### 1. Tree size

Size is the starting point. Taller trees with wider trunks and big crowns take more time to cut, more rigging to lower safely and more work to chip and haul. A small ornamental tree might take an hour or two. A large pecan or live oak can take a full day or more.

### 2. Location and what's around the tree

This is often just as important as size. A tree in an open field can sometimes be dropped in one piece. A tree that leans over your roof, fence, pool or a neighbor's yard must be taken down in small sections and lowered with ropes. That takes more crew, more time and more skill.

### 3. Power lines

Trees close to power lines need extra care. In many cases, your electric company must handle branches touching their lines first. Working near lines adds time and safety steps.

### 4. Access for equipment

If a truck, lift or chipper can reach the tree, the job moves faster. If the tree is in a fenced backyard with a narrow gate, on a slope or behind a pool, more of the work is done by climbing and carrying wood out by hand.

### 5. The tree's condition

Healthy wood is predictable. Dead, rotten or storm-damaged trees can be brittle and unsafe to climb, which may call for a lift or crane and slower, more careful work. Trees with oak wilt also need proper handling so the disease doesn't spread. Read more on our [oak wilt page](/oak-wilt/).

### 6. Cleanup and wood disposal

Most quotes include chipping branches and hauling them away. Large trunk wood is heavy, and hauling it adds to the cost. If you want to keep the wood for firewood, or leave logs in a back corner, that can lower the price.

### 7. Emergency situations

A tree lying on a house or blocking a driveway after a storm is a more complex, higher-risk job than a planned removal. It may also need to happen at night or in bad weather. See our [emergency tree service](/emergency-tree-service/) page for more.

## What affects stump grinding cost

Stump grinding is usually quoted separately from removal. Price depends on:

- **Stump diameter** at ground level, the biggest factor.
- **Number of stumps.** Grinding several at once usually costs less per stump.
- **Wood type.** Dense woods like oak and mesquite grind slower.
- **Access.** Tight gates and slopes slow the work.
- **Surface roots** you want ground out too.
- **Chip cleanup.** Leaving the chips as mulch or hauling them away.

Learn more on our [stump grinding](/stump-grinding/) page.

## How to compare tree service quotes

When you get more than one estimate, make sure you're comparing the same job. Ask each company:

- **Is cleanup included?** Raking, blowing and hauling away all branches and wood?
- **Is stump grinding included?** If so, how deep?
- **What happens to the wood?** Hauled away, left as logs, or cut to firewood length?
- **Are you insured?** Ask for proof of liability insurance. If a worker gets hurt or your house gets damaged, you don't want to be on the hook.
- **Who does the work?** Will the company's own crew do it, or will it be subcontracted?
- **Is the quote in writing?** A written quote avoids surprises.

## Ways to save money on tree work

- **Bundle jobs.** Combine removals, trimming and stump grinding into one visit.
- **Plan ahead.** Taking care of a dead or leaning tree before a storm is usually less costly than an emergency removal after it falls.
- **Keep the wood.** If you can use firewood, ask to have it cut and left on site.
- **Trim regularly.** Routine [trimming](/tree-trimming/) can prevent storm damage and keep a good tree from needing removal later.
