## Creekside trees along Nolan Creek and the Leon River

Nolan Creek runs right through downtown Belton, and the Leon River passes nearby. Bottomland along these waterways grows big pecans, sycamores, cedar elms and bald cypress. These trees are beautiful, but they come with a few common problems:

- **Heavy pecan limbs** that grow long and can break in wind or even on calm summer days.
- **Soft, wet soil** after rain, which can make it easier for trees to lean or uproot.
- **Flood debris and storm damage** that leaves broken limbs hanging in the canopy.

If you live near the creek, regular [tree trimming](/tree-trimming/) to remove deadwood and lighten heavy limbs is one of the best ways to prevent damage.

## Lake lots and hill country edges

Neighborhoods heading toward Lake Belton and Stillhouse Hollow Lake are hillier and rockier. Here you'll see more live oaks and Ashe juniper (cedar) growing in thin soil over limestone. Common jobs include clearing cedar to open up views, removing trees on slopes and thinning brush near homes. For bigger properties, our [land clearing](/land-clearing/) service can open up overgrown areas while protecting the oaks you want to keep.

## Oak wilt and storms in Belton

Like the rest of {{county}}, Belton is in an area where oak wilt is a known risk. Live oaks on connected roots can lose several trees in a row. Learn the warning signs on our [oak wilt page](/oak-wilt/), and remember not to prune oaks from February through June.
