## Fence lines, acreage and farm trees

Out on the Blackland Prairie around Troy, trees tend to grow where people let them: along fence lines, in old windbreaks, along creeks and around homesteads. Over the years those spots fill up with hackberry, cedar, mesquite and bois d'arc. Common jobs we do on Troy acreage include:

- **Fence line clearing** so you can fix or replace fencing and keep it maintained.
- **Removing dead or dying trees** near barns, sheds, pens and driveways.
- **Clearing brush and small trees** to reclaim pasture or open space. See [land clearing](/land-clearing/).
- **Grinding stumps** so you can mow and drive equipment over the area. See [stump grinding](/stump-grinding/).

## Big yard trees in town

In town, many Troy homes have big pecans, cedar elms and hackberries that shade the house and yard. These trees are worth keeping healthy. Removing deadwood, raising low limbs and cutting branches back from the roof with regular [tree trimming](/tree-trimming/) reduces the chance of storm damage and keeps your yard usable.

## Open land means wind

On open prairie, there's not much to slow the wind. Trees standing alone or along fence rows take the full force of spring storms and strong north winds. Heavy clay soil that's soaked from rain can also let trees tip over more easily. If a storm drops a tree on your home, fence or driveway, call {{phone}} for [emergency tree service](/emergency-tree-service/).

## Oak wilt and Troy oaks

Oaks are less common on open prairie than in the hills to the west, but many Troy properties still have live oaks and red oaks worth protecting. The same oak wilt rules apply here: don't prune oaks from February through June, and paint fresh cuts right away. Learn more on our [oak wilt care](/oak-wilt/) page.
