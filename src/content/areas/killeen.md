Killeen is the biggest city in {{county}} and sits right next to Fort Cavazos. That means a lot of families come and go, a lot of homes are rentals and many neighborhoods are newer. Tree needs here are a little different from older towns, and we've built our service to fit.

## Newer neighborhoods and young trees

Many Killeen subdivisions were built in the last few decades. Builders often planted the same few fast-growing trees in every yard, like Bradford pears, Chinese pistache and young live oaks. As these trees mature, a few issues show up:

- **Bradford pears splitting.** These trees grow narrow forks that often break apart in wind or ice. When a pear starts to split, [removal](/tree-removal/) is usually the safest choice.
- **Young trees with poor structure.** Early pruning helps trees grow one strong trunk instead of weak, competing ones.
- **Trees planted too close to the house.** A cute little tree can be hanging over the roof ten years later. Planned [trimming](/tree-trimming/) keeps it in check.

## Rocky soil, cedar and hill country edges

The western and southern sides of Killeen run into rocky, hilly ground with thin soil over limestone. Ashe juniper (cedar) and live oaks grow well here, and cedar can take over a backyard fast. We clear cedar, cut and grind stumps and thin brush near homes to cut down on clutter and wildfire fuel in dry summers. For larger tracts, see our [land clearing](/land-clearing/) service.

## Storms and oak wilt in Killeen

Killeen gets the same strong spring thunderstorms, high winds and hail as the rest of Central Texas. Thin, rocky soil can make it harder for some trees to stay anchored in a big wind. If a tree comes down on your home, car or fence, call {{phone}} for [emergency tree service](/emergency-tree-service/).

Oak wilt is also present in the area. Live oaks and red oaks should not be pruned from February through June unless absolutely needed, and any fresh cut should be painted. Read more about [oak wilt care](/oak-wilt/).
