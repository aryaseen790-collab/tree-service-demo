Salado is one of the most charming small towns in Central Texas. Main Street's shops and galleries, the historic Stagecoach Inn and the cool, clear water of Salado Creek draw visitors from all over. Much of that charm comes from trees: big live oaks shading old stone buildings, pecans and cypress along the creek and wooded lots with plenty of privacy. We're a short drive down I-35 from {{city}}, and we love working on Salado's trees.

## Heritage live oaks need a lighter touch

Some of Salado's live oaks have been growing for generations. A tree like that can't be replaced in our lifetime, so every cut matters. When we work on older oaks, we:

- **Remove only what's needed**, like deadwood, broken limbs and branches rubbing on roofs.
- **Make clean cuts** at the right spot so the tree can seal the wound.
- **Avoid topping** or heavy thinning that stresses an old tree.
- **Work in the safer season** for oaks whenever we can, and paint fresh cuts right away.

Learn more about how we prune on our [tree trimming page](/tree-trimming/).

## Oak wilt in a town full of oaks

Because Salado has so many live oaks, oak wilt is a real concern. Live oaks often share connected roots, so one infected tree can lead to others nearby. If you see live oak leaves with yellow or brown veins, heavy leaf drop or several oaks going downhill in the same area, call us. Our [oak wilt care](/oak-wilt/) page explains the signs and what can be done.

## Salado Creek and wooded lots

Along Salado Creek and in neighborhoods with large wooded lots, the growth can get thick. Ashe juniper (cedar) and understory brush crowd out good trees, block views and add fuel in a dry summer. Selective [land clearing](/land-clearing/) removes cedar and brush while keeping your best oaks, elms and pecans. Creek banks need extra care so cut material stays out of the water and soil isn't left bare.
