## Temple trees and the Blackland Prairie

Temple sits on the Blackland Prairie, known for its deep, dark clay soil. That soil is great for farming, but it can be hard on trees. Clay dries out and cracks during long droughts, then turns soft and heavy after big rains. Large shade trees like pecans and live oaks can be stressed by these swings. After a heavy spring storm, saturated clay is one reason you see whole trees lean or tip over instead of just losing a branch.

That's why we pay attention to more than just the branches. During an estimate, we look at how the tree is leaning, whether the soil is heaving at the base and whether roots have been cut by past work on driveways, sidewalks or utilities.

## Older neighborhoods, bigger trees

Many of Temple's established neighborhoods have trees that are older than the houses around them. These big oaks, elms and pecans give great shade, but they also tend to hang over roofs and power drops and grow into each other. Common Temple jobs include:

- **Crown raising** to lift limbs off roofs and give clearance over driveways and streets.
- **Deadwood removal** in mature pecans and oaks, which often drop dead limbs in storms.
- **Removing short-lived trees** like Bradford pears and Arizona ash that have started to split or decline.
- **Stump grinding** after a tree is removed, so you can replant or lay new sod.
