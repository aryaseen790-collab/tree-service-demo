## What counts as a tree emergency?

Call us right away if:

- **A tree or large limb has hit your house**, garage, car or fence.
- **A tree is blocking your driveway** or your only way in or out.
- **A tree is cracked, split or leaning hard** after a storm and could fall on something.
- **Big broken limbs are hanging** in the canopy over your roof, yard or a play area.
- **Roots have lifted** out of wet ground and the tree is tipping.

If a tree is touching a power line, call 911 and your electric company first. Once the line is safe, call us.

## How emergency tree removal works

1. **You call.** We ask a few quick questions about what happened, where the tree is and whether anyone is hurt or in danger.
2. **We check the scene.** When we arrive, we look for hazards like power lines, branches under tension and damage to your roof.
3. **Make it safe.** We take the weight off your home first, cutting the tree in small, controlled pieces and lowering them with ropes, rigging or a crane if the job calls for it. [CONFIRM: crane access or partners]
4. **Remove the tree.** Once the danger is gone, we cut up and haul away the trunk and limbs.
5. **Clean up.** We clear your yard, driveway and gutters of branches and leaves.
6. **Help with insurance.** We can provide photos and a written description of the work for your claim.

## Why removing a tree from a house takes skill

A tree lying on a roof is not like a tree standing in a yard. The wood is often bent or twisted, which means it's loaded with stored energy. Cut in the wrong place and a limb can snap back or the tree can roll and cause more damage. This is why we strongly advise against cutting it yourself. Our crew plans each cut to take weight off the house a little at a time.

## What affects the cost of emergency tree work

Emergency jobs vary a lot, so we don't give prices over the phone without seeing the scene. Cost depends on:

- **Where the tree landed.** A tree on a roof needs more care and rigging than one across a lawn.
- **Size and weight** of the tree and how many limbs are involved.
- **Equipment needed**, such as a bucket truck, crane or extra crew.
- **Access** to the site, especially if other trees or debris block the way.
- **Time of day and conditions.** Night work or work during ongoing storms is harder and slower.

Many homeowner's policies help pay for removing a tree that hit a covered structure. Your insurer will explain your coverage.

## After the emergency

Once the danger is handled, it's smart to look at your other trees. Storms often weaken trees that are still standing. We can check them and suggest [trimming](/tree-trimming/) to reduce future risk, or [stump grinding](/stump-grinding/) for the tree that fell.
