After a tree comes down, the stump often stays behind. At first it doesn't seem like a big deal. But stumps get in the way of mowing, catch kids' feet and can send up new sprouts for years. As they rot, they can also attract termites, carpenter ants and fungus. Stump grinding is the fastest way to get rid of a stump for good.

## When stump grinding makes sense

Think about grinding when:

- **You keep mowing around it** or have hit it with the mower.
- **New sprouts keep popping up** from the stump or roots.
- **It's in the way** of new sod, a garden bed, a fence or a patio.
- **It's starting to rot** and you're seeing bugs, fungus or soft wood.
- **You're selling your home** and want the yard to look clean and finished.
- **You just had a tree removed** and want the job done all the way. We can grind on the same visit as [tree removal](/tree-removal/).

## Grinding vs. full stump removal

Stump grinding uses a machine with a spinning wheel of sharp teeth. It chews the stump into chips, down below ground level. Full stump removal means digging out the stump and the large roots, which tears up much more of your yard and costs more. For most homeowners, grinding is the better choice. It's faster, cleaner and leaves the area ready for soil and grass.

## How stump grinding works

1. **Look at the stump.** We check the size, the area around it and how to get the grinder in.
2. **Mark utilities.** We have public underground lines marked before grinding. Please point out any sprinkler lines or yard lights.
3. **Clear the area.** Rocks and debris around the stump are removed so they don't get thrown.
4. **Grind it down.** The grinder works back and forth across the stump until it's below ground.
5. **Handle the chips.** We fill the hole with the chips or haul them away, whichever you choose.
6. **Final check.** We rake the area smooth so it's ready for topsoil and seed or sod.

## After the stump is gone

The wood chips break down over time and the spot may settle a little. For grass, we suggest pulling out most of the chips, filling with good topsoil and then laying sod or seed. If you plan to plant a new tree, choose a spot a few feet away from the old stump so the new roots aren't fighting old wood.

## What affects the cost of stump grinding

Stump grinding is usually priced per stump, but the final number depends on a few things:

- **Stump width.** Measured across the widest part at ground level. Wider stumps take longer.
- **Number of stumps.** Several stumps on one visit often cost less per stump than one at a time.
- **Type of wood.** Hard woods like oak and mesquite take longer to grind than softer trees.
- **Access.** Tight gates, steep slopes or stumps near walls and fences slow the work.
- **Roots.** Grinding large surface roots adds time.
- **Cleanup.** Hauling away all the chips is extra work compared to leaving them as mulch.
