Oak wilt is a disease caused by a fungus that blocks the water-carrying tissue inside oak trees. It has killed huge numbers of oaks across Central Texas, and {{county}} is right in the middle of the area where it's found. Live oaks and red oaks are hit the hardest, and both are common in yards around {{city}}. The good news: knowing the signs and following a few simple rules can make a big difference.

## How oak wilt spreads

Oak wilt moves in two main ways:

- **Through roots.** Live oaks often grow in groups with connected roots. Once one tree is infected, the fungus can move through those roots to nearby trees. This is why oak wilt often spreads in a slowly growing circle.
- **By beetles.** Infected red oaks can grow mats of fungus under their bark. Small sap beetles feed on these mats, then fly to fresh wounds on healthy oaks and carry the fungus with them. This is why spring pruning of oaks is risky.

## Signs of oak wilt

Watch for these, especially in spring and early summer:

- **Live oaks:** leaves with yellow or brown veins (called "veinal necrosis"), leaves dropping in large amounts, and the crown thinning out over a few months.
- **Red oaks (like Texas red oak and Shumard oak):** leaves turning dull green, then red-brown from the edges in, often in early summer. The whole tree can die within weeks.
- **A group pattern:** several oaks near each other declining one after another.

Drought and other problems can cause similar signs, so it's worth having a pro take a look before you decide anything.

## How to help prevent oak wilt

- **Don't prune oaks from February through June.** If you must, paint the cut right away.
- **Paint all fresh oak wounds**, including ones from storms or lawn equipment.
- **Be careful with firewood.** Don't bring in unseasoned oak firewood from unknown sources.
- **Hire tree workers who know oak wilt.** Tools and timing matter. Ask how they handle oaks.
